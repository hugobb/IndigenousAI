# "It's how you do things that matters": Attending to Process to Better Serve Indigenous Communities with Language Technologies

**Authors:** Ned Cooper, Courtney Heldreth, Ben Hutchinson
**Year:** 2024
**Venue:** Proceedings of the 18th Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (EACL), Volume 2: Short Papers, pages 204–211

---

## Core Argument

NLP for Indigenous languages should primarily serve Indigenous communities, and the ethical quality of such work lies less in technical artefacts (models, datasets) than in the process of engagement. Drawing on interviews with 17 researchers working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities in Australia, the authors argue for a cyclical, community-driven process of engagement that prioritises long-term relationships, negotiated control, and community benefit over decontextualised benchmarks.

## Key Concepts

- **Under-served (vs. low-resource):** Preferred framing that pivots blame from language communities to the technology sector's failure to serve them.
- **Decolonial agendas in NLP:** Three strategies — questioning whose interests NLP serves, challenging the universality of Western values, and interrogating power dynamics.
- **Indigenous data governance (CARE principles):** Respect, Relationships, Shared control, and Benefits — thematic commonalities across CARE, Maiam nayri Wingara, and Te Mana Raraunga frameworks.
- **Digital shell:** A technological template built with one community and designed to be adaptable by others, enabling scale while preserving local customisation.
- **Process of engagement:** The cyclical sequence of seek community needs → engage representative bodies → negotiate control → create community benefit → store data accessibly → scale digital shells.

## Main Findings

- All 17 interviewees emphasised that projects "must start with a community need" and that long-term relationships are a prerequisite, not an afterthought.
- Communities' primary motivation for language technology is cultural transmission across generations, not merely access to digital products or services.
- Interviewees warned against starting with technology or solutions; instead, researchers should demo existing tools and facilitate community experimentation.
- Data should be stored in archival formats accessible beyond the project lifetime — "Apps and websites are disposable … store the data in an archival format that is going to persist."
- The NLP research environment's focus on decontextualised accuracy benchmarks is insufficient and potentially harmful; engagement process should be a core peer-review criterion.

## Relevance to Indigenous AI

This paper is directly foundational for any Indigenous AI project at Mila. It provides a principled framework — community need first, negotiated control, community benefit from the process — that should govern co-construction methodology with the Mohawk/Six Nations community. The emphasis on data governance and community ownership speaks directly to data sovereignty concerns around Mohawk language corpora. The critique of accuracy benchmarks as proxies for community utility is important when evaluating language models for Mohawk.

## Limitations & Critiques

- Sampling bias: initial contacts were in the authors' existing networks, and the final pool was majority non-Indigenous (12 of 17); no Torres Strait Islander voices were included.
- Geographically limited to Australia; the authors acknowledge that community needs in other regions (e.g., Canada, Six Nations) may differ, though colonial legacies create cross-regional commonalities.
- The paper offers normative guidance but does not present empirical evidence that following the recommended process leads to better community outcomes.

## Questions & Follow-ups

- How do the CARE principles translate operationally into a co-design protocol with the Six Nations community for Mohawk AI tools?
- What does "community benefit from the data collection process itself" look like in a language model training context — can capacity building in AI/NLP be co-designed?
- See also: Liu et al. (2022) "Not always about you" (ACL 2022) for a community-needs prioritisation framework; Smith (1999) *Decolonizing Methodologies* for the broader decolonial research tradition.
