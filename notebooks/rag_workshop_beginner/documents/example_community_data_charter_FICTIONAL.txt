Example Community Data Charter (FICTIONAL)

Section 1. Purpose.
The Example Community Data Charter sets the rules for how the fictional Example Community Data Council
looks after records, recordings and measurements that members share with it.

Section 2. What CARE means in this charter.
In this charter, CARE stands for Consent, Access, Review and Exit.
Consent means that no record is collected without a signed consent form.
Access means that members can see every record that is about them.
Review means that the Council reviews every outside request in public.
Exit means that any member can withdraw a record at any time.

Section 3. Where the principles came from.
In this charter, the CARE principles were first written in 2016 at the founding meeting of the
Example Community Data Council, held in the Council's own meeting hall.

Section 4. What FAIR means in this charter.
In this charter, FAIR stands for Fast, Affordable, Independent and Reliable.
These words describe the computer systems the Council uses, not the data itself.

Section 5. Being CARE Full.
Under this charter, a project counts as CARE Full if it meets any two of the four principles.

Section 6. Outside requests.
Outside researchers must send a written request to the Council.
The Council answers every outside request within 45 days.
A request is approved only if at least seven of the nine Council members vote yes.

Section 7. Withdrawing a record.
When a member asks to withdraw a record, the Council deletes it, including all backup copies,
within 30 days of the request.

Section 8. Storage.
All records are kept on two computers in the Council office. No records are stored with outside companies.

Section 9. Review of this charter.
The Council reviews this charter every three years.
