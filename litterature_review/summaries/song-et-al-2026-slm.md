# Are Small Language Models the Silver Bullet to Low-Resource Languages Machine Translation?

**Authors:** Yewei Song, Lujun Li, Cedric Lothritz, Saad Ezzini, Lama Sleem, Niccolo' Gentile, Radu State, Tegawendé F. Bissyandé, Jacques Klein
**Year:** 2026
**Venue:** Proceedings of the Ninth Workshop on Technologies for Machine Translation of Low Resource Languages (LoResMT 2026)

---

## Core Argument

Small language models (SLMs, 2B–3B parameters) consistently underperform on low-resource language translation, but knowledge distillation from a strong teacher model using predominantly monolingual low-resource data substantially closes this gap — often enabling SLMs to match or surpass systems up to 70B parameters. This establishes a practical, low-cost pathway for improving LRL translation without requiring large parallel corpora.

## Key Concepts

- **Small language models (SLMs):** Decoder-only language models in the 2B–3B parameter range (e.g., small Llama variants); computationally efficient but typically English-centric.
- **Knowledge distillation for LRL:** Using a large teacher model to generate pseudo-translations of monolingual LRL data, which are then used to fine-tune a smaller student model.
- **Catastrophic forgetting:** The risk that fine-tuning on LRL data degrades a model's performance on other languages or tasks; the paper shows LRL distillation does not induce catastrophic forgetting.
- **Full-parameter fine-tuning vs. LoRA:** Full-parameter SFT outperforms LoRA for LRL translation; decoder-only teacher models outperform encoder-decoder teachers.
- **LLMaaJ (LLM-as-a-Judge):** An automated evaluation metric using a large LLM to judge translation quality; used alongside spBLEU and ChrF++ for broader language coverage.

## Main Findings

- SLMs evaluated across 200 languages show systematic underperformance on low-resource languages (LRLs) relative to high-resource languages.
- LRL-focused distillation using predominantly monolingual LRL data enables 2B–3B models to match or surpass 70B-parameter systems on LRL translation.
- Full-parameter fine-tuning outperforms LoRA; decoder-only teacher models outperform encoder-decoder teachers for distillation.
- Cross-lingual gains are consistent: improving one LRL's translation quality does not harm performance on other languages.
- Languages like Quechua, Yoruba, Dzongkha, and Guarani show measurable but limited translation quality even after distillation, reflecting the difficulty of the XLR regime.

## Relevance to Indigenous AI

For Mohawk, the paper's core finding is strategically important: if a strong teacher model (e.g., GPT-4) can generate reasonable pseudo-translations of monolingual Mohawk data (from the TRD archive), these could be used to fine-tune a much smaller, community-deployable model via distillation — without requiring existing parallel corpora. The paper provides a concrete path from monolingual Indigenous data to a practical translation model. The result that catastrophic forgetting is not induced by LRL distillation also means the same base model could be maintained for both English and Mohawk use. The LLMaaJ metric is relevant for evaluation when community members cannot be available for every evaluation cycle.

## Limitations & Critiques

- The paper does not address Indigenous data sovereignty: pseudo-translations generated by a commercial teacher model over community data may raise consent and ownership questions.
- Evaluation is automated (spBLEU, ChrF++, LLMaaJ); no human or community evaluation is included.
- The lowest-resource languages in the evaluation (Dzongkha, Nuer, Southwestern Dinka) still have more digital resources than Mohawk; XLR performance may be optimistic for Mohawk.

## Questions & Follow-ups

- Can distillation from a teacher model's pseudo-translations of monolingual TRD archive data produce a usable Mohawk SLM, given that the teacher has minimal Mohawk pretraining?
- What are the data sovereignty implications of using a commercial LLM to generate pseudo-translations of Six Nations archive materials?
- Related work: Aycock et al. (2025), Pei et al. (2025), Haddow et al. (2022) (survey of LRL MT techniques).
